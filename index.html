Research & Engineering

My work sits between design and the lab. Before tape-out, I build verification environments that try hard to break the chip. After silicon comes back, I bring it up, characterize it and chase down whatever doesn’t match. Most of these chips run at cryogenic temperatures for detectors and quantum systems, built with collaborators at NASA, Caltech, MIT, CERN and Microsoft.

SUNROC: a 32-channel cryogenic readout ASIC for SNSPD arrays

RTL design · UVM verification · FPGA bring-up

SUNROC reads out arrays of superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors at 4 K, with 32 readout channels, four 1 Gbps SerDes links and a 10 GHz PLL. I designed its digital readout in SystemVerilog: fine-delay TDCs with bubble-error filtering, multi-channel priority arbiters and high-speed CML serializers. The configuration registers use triple modular redundancy with live SEU counters, and slow control runs over a custom GPPI link and an I²C-to-Wishbone bridge. I verified the blocks with UVM in Fermilab’s Songbird framework, then led system-level testing on a Zynq UltraScale+ (ZCU102) board with PetaLinux and AXI firmware, correlating silicon against Cocotb and Xcelium simulations.

Related: IEEE NSS/MIC 2024 paper and TWEPP 2025 talk

Bringing a cryogenic PLL back to lock

Silicon debug · phase-noise measurement

On silicon at 4 K, the PLL would not lock. Working through VCO and loop analysis, I retuned the VCO sub-bands and loop parameters until it locked stably, with 1.875 ps RMS jitter.

Related paper: A 10 GHz low-jitter cryogenic PLL for quantum applications

SPROCKET: SAR ADC readout for Skipper-CCD sensors

Mixed-signal verification · cryogenic validation

I verified the chip for tape-out, then validated it on the bench down to 77 K, correlating measured silicon with simulation so there were fewer surprises after fabrication.

Related paper: A cryogenic readout IC with 100 KSPS in-pixel ADC

Spacely: open-source test automation

Python · lab automation

A Python framework (1,000+ lines) for SMU and AWG instrument control, data acquisition and analysis. It made 28 nm FDSOI transistor characterization about 80% faster and more repeatable.
